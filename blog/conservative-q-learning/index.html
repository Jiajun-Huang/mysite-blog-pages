These notes follow the conservative Q-learning objective from its tabular update to the loss used in a continuous-action critic. The distinction between a lower bound on every action value and a lower bound on a policy’s expected value matters throughout the derivation.

Notation and the source of the error

Let dD(s)d_{\mathcal D}(s)dD​(s) denote the dataset’s state marginal and let β(a∣s)=π^β(a∣s)\beta(a|s)=\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)β(a∣s)=π^β​(a∣s) denote its empirical action probabilities. In the tabular calculations below, assume dD(s)>0d_{\mathcal D}(s)>0dD​(s)>0 and β(a∣s)>0\beta(a|s)>0β(a∣s)>0 for each state-action pair being analyzed. This is an analysis assumption, not a description of sparse continuous-action data.

For a fixed policy π\piπ, its Bellman operator is

(BπQ)(s,a)=r(s,a)+γ∑s′P(s′∣s,a)∑a′π(a′∣s′)Q(s′,a′).(\mathcal B^\pi Q)(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)\sum_{a'}\pi(a'|s')Q(s',a').(BπQ)(s,a)=r(s,a)+γs′∑​P(s′∣s,a)a′∑​π(a′∣s′)Q(s′,a′).

The dataset can constrain the left-hand side only at observed actions. The expectation on the right can still evaluate poorly supported actions. A small training TD error therefore does not establish that the policy’s value estimate is accurate outside the dataset.

For example, suppose the true values of two actions are Qπ(s,a1)=1Q^\pi(s,a_1)=1Qπ(s,a1​)=1 and Qπ(s,a2)=0Q^\pi(s,a_2)=0Qπ(s,a2​)=0, but the dataset contains only a1a_1a1​. A critic predicting Q^(s,a1)=1\hat Q(s,a_1)=1Q^​(s,a1​)=1 and Q^(s,a2)=10\hat Q(s,a_2)=10Q^​(s,a2​)=10 can fit observed targets while inducing a policy that selects a2a_2a2​. This example illustrates extrapolation error; the tabular lower-bound derivations below require stronger coverage than this example provides.

Offline RL and the conservative objective

Offline RL learns from a fixed dataset D\mathcal DD collected by a behavior policy πβ\pi_\betaπβ​. Bellman targets query actions selected by the learned policy, although those actions may have little data support. Function approximation can overestimate their values; policy improvement then favors these actions and bootstrapping propagates the error. New interactions cannot correct it. CQL regularizes the critic rather than requiring an explicit behavior-policy model or constraint.

For a policy πk\pi_kπk​, write yk=r+γEa′∼πk(⋅∣s′)Q^k(s′,a′)y_k=r+\gamma\mathbb E_{a'\sim\pi_k(\cdot|s')}\hat Q^k(s',a')yk​=r+γEa′∼πk​(⋅∣s′)​Q^​k(s′,a′) and LTD(Q)=12ED(Q(s,a)−yk)2\mathcal L_{\rm TD}(Q)=\frac12\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}(Q(s,a)-y_k)^2LTD​(Q)=21​ED​(Q(s,a)−yk​)2. Paper Eq. (1) adds αEs∼D,a∼μQ(s,a)\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D,a\sim\mu}Q(s,a)αEs∼D,a∼μ​Q(s,a), lowering values under a chosen action distribution μ\muμ. Eq. (2) tightens this conservative estimate by also raising dataset-action values:

Q^k+1=arg⁡min⁡Q{LTD(Q)+αEs∼D[Ea∼μ(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)−Ea∼π^β(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)]}.\hat Q^{k+1}=\arg\min_Q\left\{\mathcal L_{\rm TD}(Q)+\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D} \left[\mathbb E_{a\sim\mu(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a)-\mathbb E_{a\sim\hat\pi_\beta(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a)\right]\right\}.Q^​k+1=argQmin​{LTD​(Q)+αEs∼D​[Ea∼μ(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)−Ea∼π^β​(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)]}.

Here π^β\hat\pi_\betaπ^β​ is empirical: its expectation is evaluated directly on dataset actions. The coefficient α\alphaα balances Bellman fitting and conservatism.

Expanding the entropy-regularized maximization

The softmax calculation can also be written with an explicit temperature τ>0\tau>0τ>0. At a single state, solve

max⁡p∈Δ(A){∑apaqa−τ∑apalog⁡pa}.\max_{p\in\Delta(\mathcal A)}\left\{\sum_a p_a q_a-\tau\sum_a p_a\log p_a\right\}.p∈Δ(A)max​{a∑​pa​qa​−τa∑​pa​logpa​}.

qa−τ(log⁡pa+1)−ν=0,pa=exp⁡(qa−ντ−1).q_a-\tau(\log p_a+1)-\nu=0, \qquad p_a=\exp\left(\frac{q_a-\nu}{\tau}-1\right).qa​−τ(logpa​+1)−ν=0,pa​=exp(τqa​−ν​−1).

All actions share the same normalization factor, so

pa∗=eqa/τ∑beqb/τ,max⁡p{p⊤q+τH(p)}=τlog⁡∑aeqa/τ.p_a^*=\frac{e^{q_a/\tau}}{\sum_b e^{q_b/\tau}}, \qquad \max_p\{p^\top q+\tau H(p)\}=\tau\log\sum_a e^{q_a/\tau}.pa∗​=∑b​eqb​/τeqa​/τ​,pmax​{p⊤q+τH(p)}=τloga∑​eqa​/τ.

τDKL(p∥p∗)=τ∑apalog⁡pa−∑apaqa+τlog⁡Z.\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|p^*) =\tau\sum_a p_a\log p_a-\sum_a p_aq_a+\tau\log Z.τDKL​(p∥p∗)=τa∑​pa​logpa​−a∑​pa​qa​+τlogZ.

Rearranging shows that the objective equals τlog⁡Z−τDKL(p∥p∗)\tau\log Z-\tau D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|p^*)τlogZ−τDKL​(p∥p∗). Its maximum is attained at p=p∗p=p^*p=p∗ because KL divergence is nonnegative. Setting τ=1\tau=1τ=1 recovers CQL(H)‘s log-sum-exp term.

For numerical stability, compute it as

τlog⁡∑aeqa/τ=m+τlog⁡∑ae(qa−m)/τ,m=max⁡aqa.\tau\log\sum_a e^{q_a/\tau} =m+\tau\log\sum_a e^{(q_a-m)/\tau}, \qquad m=\max_a q_a.τloga∑​eqa​/τ=m+τloga∑​e(qa​−m)/τ,m=amax​qa​.

Subtracting the maximum inside the exponential changes neither the value nor its gradient. It prevents large positive critic outputs from overflowing.

What the regularizer changes

With Rs(Q)=τlog⁡∑beQ(s,b)/τ−∑bβ(b∣s)Q(s,b)R_s(Q)=\tau\log\sum_b e^{Q(s,b)/\tau}-\sum_b\beta(b|s)Q(s,b)Rs​(Q)=τlog∑b​eQ(s,b)/τ−∑b​β(b∣s)Q(s,b),

∂Rs∂Q(s,a)=eQ(s,a)/τ∑beQ(s,b)/τ−β(a∣s).\frac{\partial R_s}{\partial Q(s,a)} =\frac{e^{Q(s,a)/\tau}}{\sum_b e^{Q(s,b)/\tau}}-\beta(a|s).∂Q(s,a)∂Rs​​=∑b​eQ(s,b)/τeQ(s,a)/τ​−β(a∣s).

Gradient descent decreases an action’s value when its softmax probability exceeds its dataset frequency. It increases that value when the inequality is reversed. The TD term still contributes its own gradient, so this sign calculation describes the conservative regularizer rather than the whole update.

At a fixed state, adding a constant ccc to every action value leaves RsR_sRs​ unchanged: both terms increase by ccc. CQL(H) thus penalizes relative preferences over actions. The Bellman loss also remains necessary to anchor the absolute values.

The implemented critic objective

Substituting the entropy-regularized maximizer into the conservative objective gives CQL(H). With temperature one, its critic update is

Q^k+1=arg⁡min⁡Q{LTD(Q)+αEs∼D[log⁡∑aeQ(s,a)−Ea∼π^βQ(s,a)]}.\hat Q^{k+1}=\arg\min_Q\left\{\mathcal L_{\rm TD}(Q)+\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D}\left[\log\sum_a e^{Q(s,a)}-\mathbb E_{a\sim\hat\pi_\beta}Q(s,a)\right]\right\}.Q^​k+1=argQmin​{LTD​(Q)+αEs∼D​[loga∑​eQ(s,a)−Ea∼π^β​​Q(s,a)]}.

The first term fits the Bellman target. The bracket compares the critic’s soft maximum over actions with its value on dataset actions. CQL(ρ\rhoρ) instead uses a reference distribution in the inner optimization; the original notes discuss a previous-policy prior as a way to reduce importance-weight variance.

Discrete and continuous implementations

For discrete actions, enumerate all actions, compute a stable log-sum-exp exactly, subtract the minibatch mean of Q(s,adata)Q(s,a_{\rm data})Q(s,adata​), and add the result to the TD loss. The paper builds its discrete variant on QR-DQN.

For continuous actions, replace the sum by an integral. With proposal qqq covering the action domain,

log⁡∫eQ(s,a) da ≈ LSE⁡i=1N[Q(s,ai)−log⁡q(ai)]−log⁡N,ai∼q.\log\int e^{Q(s,a)}\,da\ \approx\ \operatorname{LSE}_{i=1}^{N}\big[Q(s,a_i)-\log q(a_i)\big]-\log N,\qquad a_i\sim q.log∫eQ(s,a)da ≈ LSEi=1N​[Q(s,ai​)−logq(ai​)]−logN,ai​∼q.

In the reference code’s importance-sampled branch (min_q_version=3\mathtt{min\_q\_version}=3min_q_version=3), each transition supplies three groups: au∼U([−1,1]d)a_u\sim U([-1,1]^d)au​∼U([−1,1]d), ac∼πϕ(⋅∣s)a_c\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot|s)ac​∼πϕ​(⋅∣s), and an∼πϕ(⋅∣s′)a_n\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot|s')an​∼πϕ​(⋅∣s′). All are evaluated at sss. Concatenate Q(s,au)−log⁡(2−d)Q(s,a_u)-\log(2^{-d})Q(s,au​)−log(2−d), Q(s,ac)−log⁡πϕ(ac∣s)Q(s,a_c)-\log\pi_\phi(a_c|s)Q(s,ac​)−logπϕ​(ac​∣s), and Q(s,an)−log⁡πϕ(an∣s′)Q(s,a_n)-\log\pi_\phi(a_n|s')Q(s,an​)−logπϕ​(an​∣s′), detaching policy log densities for the critic update. These are separate proposal corrections, not one mixture-density correction. The default temperature is one. The code takes their log-sum-exp, omits the constant −log⁡(3N)-\log(3N)−log(3N) for NNN samples per group, subtracts mean data Q, and adds the weighted penalty to each critic’s TD loss. This constant leaves critic gradients unchanged but shifts the threshold if the optional Lagrange multiplier is learned. The logarithm of a finite-sample integral estimate is generally biased.

Expanding the continuous-action approximation

For a bounded action domain A\mathcal AA, define the partition integral

ZQ(s)=∫AeQ(s,a)/τ da.Z_Q(s)=\int_{\mathcal A}e^{Q(s,a)/\tau}\,da.ZQ​(s)=∫A​eQ(s,a)/τda.

If a proposal density q(a)q(a)q(a) is positive wherever the integrand contributes, multiply and divide by qqq:

ZQ(s)=Ea∼q[eQ(s,a)/τq(a)].Z_Q(s)=\mathbb E_{a\sim q}\left[\frac{e^{Q(s,a)/\tau}}{q(a)}\right].ZQ​(s)=Ea∼q​[q(a)eQ(s,a)/τ​].

An unbiased estimate of ZQZ_QZQ​ is Z^Q=N−1∑ieQ(s,ai)/τ/q(ai)\hat Z_Q=N^{-1}\sum_i e^{Q(s,a_i)/\tau}/q(a_i)Z^Q​=N−1∑i​eQ(s,ai​)/τ/q(ai​). Taking its logarithm gives

τlog⁡Z^Q=τLSE⁡i(Q(s,ai)τ−log⁡q(ai))−τlog⁡N.\tau\log\hat Z_Q =\tau\operatorname{LSE}_i\left(\frac{Q(s,a_i)}{\tau}-\log q(a_i)\right)-\tau\log N.τlogZ^Q​=τLSEi​(τQ(s,ai​)​−logq(ai​))−τlogN.

The density correction is inside the exponential’s log argument. At temperature one this reduces to the expression above. Uniform sampling on [−1,1]d[-1,1]^d[−1,1]d has density 2−d2^{-d}2−d because the box has volume 2d2^d2d.

If each of three proposals separately covers the integration domain and contributes NNN samples, averaging their three importance estimates gives a 3N3N3N-term estimator. This explains the constant −τlog⁡(3N)-\tau\log(3N)−τlog(3N). Alternatively, samples from a mixture can be corrected by the mixture density; that is a different estimator. A proposal that misses part of the support does not become a valid full-domain importance estimator just because its log density is subtracted.

For fixed α\alphaα, omitting −τlog⁡(3N)-\tau\log(3N)−τlog(3N) adds a constant to the critic loss, so the critic gradient is unchanged. If α\alphaα is optimized against a target action gap, the multiplier sees the shifted numerical gap. Sample count, temperature and the chosen threshold then need to be considered together.

Finally, unbiasedness of Z^Q\hat Z_QZ^Q​ does not imply unbiasedness of log⁡Z^Q\log\hat Z_QlogZ^Q​. Jensen’s inequality gives

E[log⁡Z^Q]≤log⁡E[Z^Q]=log⁡ZQ.\mathbb E[\log\hat Z_Q]\leq\log\mathbb E[\hat Z_Q]=\log Z_Q.E[logZ^Q​]≤logE[Z^Q​]=logZQ​.

This sampling effect is separate from the conservatism introduced by the objective. Finite-action enumeration avoids this particular integration approximation.

Deriving the tabular lower bound

Hold the Bellman target y(s,a)y(s,a)y(s,a) fixed during one critic update. At one state, the part of Eq. (2) depending on its action values is

Js(Q)=12∑aβ(a∣s)[Q(s,a)−y(s,a)]2+α∑a[μ(a∣s)−β(a∣s)]Q(s,a).J_s(Q)=\frac12\sum_a\beta(a|s)[Q(s,a)-y(s,a)]^2 +\alpha\sum_a[\mu(a|s)-\beta(a|s)]Q(s,a).Js​(Q)=21​a∑​β(a∣s)[Q(s,a)−y(s,a)]2+αa∑​[μ(a∣s)−β(a∣s)]Q(s,a).

The state marginal multiplies both terms and cancels from the first-order condition. For each covered action,

∂Js∂Q(s,a)=β(a∣s)[Q(s,a)−y(s,a)]+α[μ(a∣s)−β(a∣s)]=0.\frac{\partial J_s}{\partial Q(s,a)} =\beta(a|s)[Q(s,a)-y(s,a)] +\alpha[\mu(a|s)-\beta(a|s)]=0.∂Q(s,a)∂Js​​=β(a∣s)[Q(s,a)−y(s,a)]+α[μ(a∣s)−β(a∣s)]=0.

Dividing by β(a∣s)\beta(a|s)β(a∣s) yields

Qnew(s,a)=y(s,a)−α(μ(a∣s)β(a∣s)−1).Q_{\mathrm{new}}(s,a)=y(s,a)-\alpha\left(\frac{\mu(a|s)}{\beta(a|s)}-1\right).Qnew​(s,a)=y(s,a)−α(β(a∣s)μ(a∣s)​−1).

This division is precisely where the coverage assumption enters. It is undefined for an unseen action with zero empirical probability; a neural network’s extrapolation to that action cannot be justified by this calculation alone.

For policy evaluation, take μ=π\mu=\piμ=π and define c(s,a)=π(a∣s)/β(a∣s)−1c(s,a)=\pi(a|s)/\beta(a|s)-1c(s,a)=π(a∣s)/β(a∣s)−1. Under an exact Bellman operator, the fixed point satisfies

cˉ(s)=∑aπ(a∣s)c(s,a)=∑aπ(a∣s)2β(a∣s)−1=∑a[π(a∣s)−β(a∣s)]2β(a∣s)≥0.\bar c(s)=\sum_a\pi(a|s)c(s,a) =\sum_a\frac{\pi(a|s)^2}{\beta(a|s)}-1 =\sum_a\frac{[\pi(a|s)-\beta(a|s)]^2}{\beta(a|s)}\geq0.cˉ(s)=a∑​π(a∣s)c(s,a)=a∑​β(a∣s)π(a∣s)2​−1=a∑​β(a∣s)[π(a∣s)−β(a∣s)]2​≥0.

A one-state check

Take a one-state MDP with γ=0\gamma=0γ=0, β=(1/2,1/2)\beta=(1/2,1/2)β=(1/2,1/2) and π=(3/4,1/4)\pi=(3/4,1/4)π=(3/4,1/4). Eq. (2) gives c=(1/2,−1/2)c=(1/2,-1/2)c=(1/2,−1/2), so

Q^(a1)=r(a1)−α/2,Q^(a2)=r(a2)+α/2.\hat Q(a_1)=r(a_1)-\alpha/2, \qquad \hat Q(a_2)=r(a_2)+\alpha/2.Q^​(a1​)=r(a1​)−α/2,Q^​(a2​)=r(a2​)+α/2.

The second action’s estimate exceeds its true value. Yet

Vπ−V^π=α[(3/4)(1/2)+(1/4)(−1/2)]=α/4≥0.V^\pi-\hat V^\pi =\alpha[(3/4)(1/2)+(1/4)(-1/2)]=\alpha/4\geq0.Vπ−V^π=α[(3/4)(1/2)+(1/4)(−1/2)]=α/4≥0.

This is an explicit example of a conservative policy value without conservative values for every action.

What sampling error changes

With an empirical operator, write e=(B^π−Bπ)Q^e=(\hat{\mathcal B}^\pi-\mathcal B^\pi)\hat Qe=(B^π−Bπ)Q^​. The fixed-point subtraction becomes

Qπ−Q^=(I−γPπ)−1(αc−e).Q^\pi-\hat Q=(I-\gamma P^\pi)^{-1}(\alpha c-e).Qπ−Q^​=(I−γPπ)−1(αc−e).

Assumptions and lower-bound theorems

The basic analysis assumes a discounted MDP (0≤γ<10\leq\gamma<10≤γ<1), bounded rewards, dataset-state evaluation, sufficient action coverage, and exactly represented/solved Q-iterations. In particular, supp⁡μ⊆supp⁡π^β\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\hat\pi_\betasuppμ⊆suppπ^β​; the paper simplifies the derivation by assuming positive empirical behavior probabilities for all actions at dataset states. Empirical reward/transition errors obey high-probability concentration bounds that decrease with sample counts. The conservative coefficient must be large enough to dominate their propagated error. Sparse coverage weakens the guarantee; extensions to linear approximation and neural tangent kernels require further assumptions.

Theorem 3.1 concerns the fixed point of Eq. (1): sufficiently large α\alphaα gives a pointwise lower bound Q^π(s,a)≤Qπ(s,a)\hat Q^\pi(s,a)\leq Q^\pi(s,a)Q^​π(s,a)≤Qπ(s,a) on the stated domain. Theorem 3.2 concerns Eq. (2) with μ=π\mu=\piμ=π: the tighter guarantee is V^π(s)=EπQ^π(s,a)≤Vπ(s)\hat V^\pi(s)=\mathbb E_\pi\hat Q^\pi(s,a)\leq V^\pi(s)V^π(s)=Eπ​Q^​π(s,a)≤Vπ(s), and need not hold for each action. The underlying tabular update illustrates why:

Q^k+1=B^πQ^k−α(ππ^β−1),Eπ[ππ^β−1]=∑aπ(a∣s)2π^β(a∣s)−1≥0.\hat Q^{k+1}=\hat{\mathcal B}^{\pi}\hat Q^k-\alpha\left(\frac{\pi}{\hat\pi_\beta}-1\right), \qquad \mathbb E_\pi\left[\frac{\pi}{\hat\pi_\beta}-1\right] =\sum_a\frac{\pi(a|s)^2}{\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)}-1\geq0.Q^​k+1=B^πQ^​k−α(π^β​π​−1),Eπ​[π^β​π​−1]=a∑​π^β​(a∣s)π(a∣s)2​−1≥0.

Experimental results and baseline comparisons

The paper’s D4RL results use normalized returns averaged over four seeds. On walker2d-medium-expert, CQL(H) scores 98.7 versus BC 11.3, BEAR 10.8, offline SAC 1.9, and BRAC-v 0.9 (Table 1), supporting its usefulness on mixed behavior data. On antmaze-medium-play it reaches 61.2 while all listed baselines score zero (Table 2), suggesting useful recombination of suboptimal trajectories. Improvements are not universal: halfcheetah-expert scores 104.8 versus BEAR 108.2 and BC 107.0; pen-cloned also favors BC. CQL(ρ\rhoρ) can be more stable on high-dimensional Adroit tasks.

The discrete Atari results support data efficiency: with 1% of replay data, Breakout returns are 61.1 for CQL, 11.0 for REM, and 7.9 for QR-DQN (Table 3). Table 4 tests conservatism directly: hopper-medium’s predicted-minus-true value is -7.48 for CQL(H), versus -156.70 for Eq. (1) and +4.32 for BEAR. This supports a tighter conservative estimate, while remaining empirical evidence on selected tasks rather than an unconditional neural-network guarantee.

Reading the objective in practice

The coefficient α\alphaα controls a tradeoff: too little conservatism can leave unsupported actions attractive, while too much can suppress useful policy improvement. A low critic loss alone cannot resolve that tradeoff. Coverage, the policy update, the action proposals and the target construction all affect what the learned values mean.

The tabular derivation gives a precise reason for the lower bound: the policy-averaged penalty is nonnegative and the discounted transition operator propagates it with nonnegative weights. The neural implementation retains the objective’s structure, but exact minimization, full coverage and exact Bellman evaluation are no longer available. Its training curves therefore need to be interpreted as empirical behavior under those approximations.
