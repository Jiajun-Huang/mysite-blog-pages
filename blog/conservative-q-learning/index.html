Conservative Q-Learning

Offline RL and overestimation

Offline reinforcement learning learns a policy from a fixed dataset D\mathcal DD, without collecting new interactions. The data is collected by a behavior policy πβ\pi_\betaπβ​, but the learned policy πk\pi_kπk​ may choose different actions.

This difference matters when computing the Bellman target for policy evaluation:

yk=r+γEa′∼πk(⋅∣s′)[Q^k(s′,a′)].y_k=r+\gamma\mathbb E_{a'\sim\pi_k(\cdot|s')} [\hat Q^k(s',a')].yk​=r+γEa′∼πk​(⋅∣s′)​[Q^​k(s′,a′)].

Here, Q^k\hat Q^kQ^​k is the current Q estimate and γ\gammaγ is the discount factor. The next action a′a'a′ comes from the learned policy, while the Q-function is fitted to actions in the dataset by minimizing

LTD(Q)=12E(s,a,r,s′)∼D[(Q(s,a)−yk)2].\mathcal L_{\mathrm{TD}}(Q) = \frac12\mathbb E_{(s,a,r,s')\sim\mathcal D} \left[(Q(s,a)-y_k)^2\right].LTD​(Q)=21​E(s,a,r,s′)∼D​[(Q(s,a)−yk​)2].

Actions chosen by πk\pi_kπk​ may be poorly covered by the data, so their Q-values can be overestimated. Since the policy favors high Q-values, it may select these out-of-distribution (OOD) actions. Without new interactions, the algorithm cannot directly check their rewards and correct these errors.

The CQL objective and its derivation

CQL [1] adds a penalty to the Bellman error to reduce overestimation. Let μ(a∣s)\mu(a|s)μ(a∣s) be the action distribution under which Q-values are penalized. The paper first considers

min⁡Q  LTD(Q)+αEs∼D,  a∼μ(⋅∣s)[Q(s,a)].(1)\min_Q\; \mathcal L_{\mathrm{TD}}(Q) +\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D,\;a\sim\mu(\cdot|s)} [Q(s,a)]. \tag{1}Qmin​LTD​(Q)+αEs∼D,a∼μ(⋅∣s)​[Q(s,a)].(1)

The coefficient α\alphaα controls the strength of the penalty. CQL regularizes the critic directly, without requiring an explicit behavior-policy model or constraint.

This objective can lower values too much. To obtain a tighter bound, the paper also raises the average Q-value of dataset actions:

min⁡Q  LTD(Q)+αEs∼D[Ea∼μ(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)−Ea∼π^β(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)].(2)\min_Q\; \mathcal L_{\mathrm{TD}}(Q) +\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D} \left[ \mathbb E_{a\sim\mu(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a) -\mathbb E_{a\sim\hat\pi_\beta(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a) \right]. \tag{2}Qmin​LTD​(Q)+αEs∼D​[Ea∼μ(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)−Ea∼π^β​(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)].(2)

Here, π^β\hat\pi_\betaπ^β​ is the empirical behavior policy. Its expectation is computed by averaging Q-values of actions in the dataset. The regularizer lowers Q-values under μ\muμ and raises them under the data distribution.

To see the resulting update, temporarily use a tabular Q-function and hold the Bellman targets fixed. At a dataset state sss, abbreviate βa=π^β(a∣s)\beta_a=\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)βa​=π^β​(a∣s), μa=μ(a∣s)\mu_a=\mu(a|s)μa​=μ(a∣s), and Qa=Q(s,a)Q_a=Q(s,a)Qa​=Q(s,a). Let yˉa=(B^πkQ^k)(s,a)\bar y_a=(\hat{\mathcal B}^{\pi_k}\hat Q^k)(s,a)yˉ​a​=(B^πk​Q^​k)(s,a) be the conditional mean target under the empirical Bellman operator. Assuming βa>0\beta_a>0βa​>0, Equation (2), up to the common state weight and terms constant in QQQ, becomes

Ls(Q)=12∑aβa(Qa−yˉa)2+α∑a(μa−βa)Qa.\mathcal L_s(Q) =\frac12\sum_a\beta_a(Q_a-\bar y_a)^2 +\alpha\sum_a(\mu_a-\beta_a)Q_a.Ls​(Q)=21​a∑​βa​(Qa​−yˉ​a​)2+αa∑​(μa​−βa​)Qa​.

Differentiating each coordinate and setting its derivative to zero gives

0=βa(Qa−yˉa)+α(μa−βa),Q^k+1(s,a)=yˉa−α(μaβa−1).\begin{aligned} 0&=\beta_a(Q_a-\bar y_a)+\alpha(\mu_a-\beta_a),\\ \hat Q^{k+1}(s,a) &=\bar y_a-\alpha\left(\frac{\mu_a}{\beta_a}-1\right). \end{aligned}0Q^​k+1(s,a)​=βa​(Qa​−yˉ​a​)+α(μa​−βa​),=yˉ​a​−α(βa​μa​​−1).​

The ratio appears because the TD fit weights an action by its data frequency βa\beta_aβa​, whereas the penalty weights it by μa\mu_aμa​. If μa>βa\mu_a>\beta_aμa​>βa​, the update pushes below the Bellman target; if μa<βa\mu_a<\beta_aμa​<βa​, it pushes above it. For Equation (1), removing the data-action reward gives Q^k+1(s,a)=yˉa−αμa/βa\hat Q^{k+1}(s,a)=\bar y_a-\alpha\mu_a/\beta_aQ^​k+1(s,a)=yˉ​a​−αμa​/βa​, a downward correction for every covered action. This coordinatewise solution explains the tabular analysis; a neural critic instead optimizes shared parameters by gradient descent.

To focus the penalty on high-value actions, CQL also optimizes μ\muμ. Adding an entropy reward keeps μ\muμ from becoming too concentrated:

min⁡Qmax⁡μ  LTD(Q)+αEs∼D[Ea∼μ(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)+H(μ(⋅∣s))−Ea∼π^β(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)].\min_Q\max_\mu\; \mathcal L_{\mathrm{TD}}(Q) +\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D} \left[ \mathbb E_{a\sim\mu(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a) +\mathcal H(\mu(\cdot|s)) -\mathbb E_{a\sim\hat\pi_\beta(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a) \right].Qmin​μmax​LTD​(Q)+αEs∼D​[Ea∼μ(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)+H(μ(⋅∣s))−Ea∼π^β​(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)].

For fixed QQQ, the inner maximization can be solved separately at each state. Write qa=Q(s,a)q_a=Q(s,a)qa​=Q(s,a) and pa=μ(a∣s)p_a=\mu(a|s)pa​=μ(a∣s), with ∑apa=1\sum_a p_a=1∑a​pa​=1 and pa≥0p_a\ge0pa​≥0. Since H(p)=−∑apalog⁡pa\mathcal H(p)=-\sum_a p_a\log p_aH(p)=−∑a​pa​logpa​, the Lagrangian is

L(p,ν)=∑apaqa−∑apalog⁡pa+ν(1−∑apa).\mathcal L(p,\nu) = \sum_a p_aq_a-\sum_a p_a\log p_a +\nu\left(1-\sum_a p_a\right).L(p,ν)=a∑​pa​qa​−a∑​pa​logpa​+ν(1−a∑​pa​).

The entropy-regularized objective is strictly concave in ppp: on the simplex interior, its Hessian is −diag⁡(1/pa)-\operatorname{diag}(1/p_a)−diag(1/pa​). Thus, the stationary distribution is the unique maximizer. Setting the derivative to zero gives

qa−(log⁡pa+1)−ν=0⟹pa∗=eqae−1−ν.q_a-(\log p_a+1)-\nu=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_a^*=e^{q_a}e^{-1-\nu}.qa​−(logpa​+1)−ν=0⟹pa∗​=eqa​e−1−ν.

Normalization then gives

e−1−ν=1∑beqb,μ∗(a∣s)=eQ(s,a)∑beQ(s,b).e^{-1-\nu}=\frac{1}{\sum_b e^{q_b}}, \qquad \mu^*(a|s)=\frac{e^{Q(s,a)}}{\sum_b e^{Q(s,b)}}.e−1−ν=∑b​eqb​1​,μ∗(a∣s)=∑b​eQ(s,b)eQ(s,a)​.

Let Z(s)=∑aeQ(s,a)Z(s)=\sum_a e^{Q(s,a)}Z(s)=∑a​eQ(s,a). Because log⁡μ∗(a∣s)=Q(s,a)−log⁡Z(s)\log\mu^*(a|s)=Q(s,a)-\log Z(s)logμ∗(a∣s)=Q(s,a)−logZ(s), substituting μ∗\mu^*μ∗ into the objective gives

Eμ∗Q+H(μ∗)=∑aμ∗(a∣s)[Q(s,a)−log⁡μ∗(a∣s)]=∑aμ∗(a∣s)log⁡Z(s)=log⁡Z(s).\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_{\mu^*}Q+\mathcal H(\mu^*) &=\sum_a\mu^*(a|s) \left[Q(s,a)-\log\mu^*(a|s)\right]\\ &=\sum_a\mu^*(a|s)\log Z(s)\\ &=\log Z(s). \end{aligned}Eμ∗​Q+H(μ∗)​=a∑​μ∗(a∣s)[Q(s,a)−logμ∗(a∣s)]=a∑​μ∗(a∣s)logZ(s)=logZ(s).​

The last step uses ∑aμ∗(a∣s)=1\sum_a\mu^*(a|s)=1∑a​μ∗(a∣s)=1. We therefore obtain CQL(H), Equation (4) in the paper, which is used in the reference implementation:

Q^k+1=arg⁡min⁡Q{LTD(Q)+αEs∼D[log⁡∑aeQ(s,a)−Ea∼π^β(⋅∣s)Q(s,a)]}.(4)\boxed{ \hat Q^{k+1} = \arg\min_Q \left\{ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{TD}}(Q) +\alpha\mathbb E_{s\sim\mathcal D} \left[ \log\sum_a e^{Q(s,a)} -\mathbb E_{a\sim\hat\pi_\beta(\cdot|s)}Q(s,a) \right] \right\}. } \tag{4}Q^​k+1=argQmin​{LTD​(Q)+αEs∼D​[loga∑​eQ(s,a)−Ea∼π^β​(⋅∣s)​Q(s,a)]}.​(4)

The log-sum-exp term penalizes high Q-values, while the dataset term reduces excessive underestimation of data actions. For a fixed state, define

Rs(Q)=log⁡∑beQ(s,b)−∑bπ^β(b∣s)Q(s,b).\mathcal R_s(Q)=\log\sum_b e^{Q(s,b)} -\sum_b\hat\pi_\beta(b|s)Q(s,b).Rs​(Q)=logb∑​eQ(s,b)−b∑​π^β​(b∣s)Q(s,b).

Differentiating the logarithm and then the data term gives

∂Rs∂Q(s,a)=1∑beQ(s,b)eQ(s,a)−π^β(a∣s)=μ∗(a∣s)−π^β(a∣s).\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\mathcal R_s}{\partial Q(s,a)} &=\frac{1}{\sum_b e^{Q(s,b)}}e^{Q(s,a)}-\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)\\ &=\mu^*(a|s)-\hat\pi_\beta(a|s). \end{aligned}∂Q(s,a)∂Rs​​​=∑b​eQ(s,b)1​eQ(s,a)−π^β​(a∣s)=μ∗(a∣s)−π^β​(a∣s).​

Gradient descent subtracts this derivative. An action with μ∗(a∣s)>π^β(a∣s)\mu^*(a|s)>\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)μ∗(a∣s)>π^β​(a∣s) is pushed down, while one with the reverse inequality is pushed up. The penalty acts on relative action values: adding the same constant ccc to all Q(s,⋅)Q(s,\cdot)Q(s,⋅) increases both terms of Rs\mathcal R_sRs​ by ccc, leaving their difference unchanged. The TD term anchors the overall value level. Choosing a previous-policy prior instead of a uniform prior gives CQL(ρ\rhoρ), which can reduce importance-weight variance.

Discrete and continuous actions

For discrete actions, all actions can be enumerated. The log-sum-exp term is therefore computed exactly, and the dataset term is estimated by averaging the Q-values of dataset actions. A numerically stable log-sum-exp is used in practice. The paper builds its discrete variant on QR-DQN.

Given a minibatch of states and dataset actions, and fixed Bellman targets, the discrete critic update is:

Plain text
q_data = Q_theta(states, data_actions)
q_all = Q_theta(states, all_actions)
penalty = mean(logsumexp(q_all, over=actions) - q_data)
critic_loss = TD_loss(q_data, stop_gradient(targets)) + alpha * penalty
theta = theta - critic_lr * gradient(critic_loss, theta)

For continuous actions, the sum becomes an integral. To derive the importance-sampling estimator, write Z(s)=∫eQ(s,a) daZ(s)=\int e^{Q(s,a)}\,daZ(s)=∫eQ(s,a)da and multiply and divide the integrand by a proposal density q(a∣s)q(a|s)q(a∣s):

Z(s)=∫q(a∣s)eQ(s,a)q(a∣s) da=Ea∼q(⋅∣s)[eQ(s,a)−log⁡q(a∣s)].\begin{aligned} Z(s) &=\int q(a|s)\frac{e^{Q(s,a)}}{q(a|s)}\,da\\ &=\mathbb E_{a\sim q(\cdot|s)} \left[e^{Q(s,a)-\log q(a|s)}\right]. \end{aligned}Z(s)​=∫q(a∣s)q(a∣s)eQ(s,a)​da=Ea∼q(⋅∣s)​[eQ(s,a)−logq(a∣s)].​

This is where the subtraction of log⁡q\log qlogq comes from. Without it, sampling from qqq estimates ∫q(a∣s)eQ(s,a) da\int q(a|s)e^{Q(s,a)}\,da∫q(a∣s)eQ(s,a)da instead of Z(s)Z(s)Z(s). Replacing the expectation by a sample average and then taking the logarithm gives

log⁡∫eQ(s,a) da≈log⁡[1N∑i=1NeQ(s,ai)−log⁡q(ai∣s)],ai∼q(⋅∣s).\log\int e^{Q(s,a)}\,da \approx \log\left[ \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N} e^{Q(s,a_i)-\log q(a_i|s)} \right], \qquad a_i\sim q(\cdot|s).log∫eQ(s,a)da≈log[N1​i=1∑N​eQ(s,ai​)−logq(ai​∣s)],ai​∼q(⋅∣s).

Here, q(a∣s)q(a|s)q(a∣s) is the action sampling density, which must cover the action domain. The density correction allows sampled actions to estimate the integral. Although the importance-sampled integral can be unbiased, taking its logarithm generally introduces finite-sample bias. In particular, for a positive unbiased estimator Z^\hat ZZ^, concavity of log⁡\loglog gives E[log⁡Z^]≤log⁡E[Z^]=log⁡Z\mathbb E[\log\hat Z]\leq\log\mathbb E[\hat Z]=\log ZE[logZ^]≤logE[Z^]=logZ. The computed expression can also be written as LSE⁡i[Q(s,ai)−log⁡q(ai∣s)]−log⁡N\operatorname{LSE}_i[Q(s,a_i)-\log q(a_i|s)]-\log NLSEi​[Q(s,ai​)−logq(ai​∣s)]−logN, matching the pseudocode below.

The continuous critic update replaces the exact action sum with this estimate:

The reference implementation [2] uses a more specific sampling scheme in its importance-sampled branch (min_q_version=3\mathtt{min\_q\_version}=3min_q_version=3). For each transition, it samples NNN actions from each of three proposals:

au∼U([−1,1]d),ac∼πϕ(⋅∣s),an∼πϕ(⋅∣s′).a_u\sim U([-1,1]^d),\qquad a_c\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot|s),\qquad a_n\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot|s').au​∼U([−1,1]d),ac​∼πϕ​(⋅∣s),an​∼πϕ​(⋅∣s′).

All sampled actions are evaluated by the critic at the current state sss. The scores concatenated before log-sum-exp are

These use separate proposal-density corrections rather than one mixture-density correction. Policy log densities are detached during the critic update. With default temperature one, the code takes log-sum-exp over the concatenated scores, subtracts the mean data-action Q-value, and adds the weighted penalty to each critic’s TD loss. It omits the normalization constant −log⁡(3N)-\log(3N)−log(3N). This omission leaves critic gradients unchanged, but shifts the threshold when the optional Lagrange multiplier is learned.

Lower-bound guarantees

The basic analysis assumes a discounted MDP with 0≤γ<10\leq\gamma<10≤γ<1, bounded rewards, evaluation at dataset states, sufficient action coverage, and exactly represented and solved Q-iterations. In particular,

supp⁡μ(⋅∣s)⊆supp⁡π^β(⋅∣s).\operatorname{supp}\mu(\cdot|s) \subseteq\operatorname{supp}\hat\pi_\beta(\cdot|s).suppμ(⋅∣s)⊆suppπ^β​(⋅∣s).

The paper simplifies the derivation by assuming positive empirical behavior probabilities for all actions at dataset states. Empirical reward and transition errors satisfy high-probability concentration bounds that decrease with sample counts. The conservative coefficient α\alphaα must be large enough to dominate these errors after Bellman propagation. Sparse coverage weakens the guarantee, and extensions to linear approximation and neural tangent kernels require further assumptions.

Under the paper’s assumptions, with a sufficiently strong conservative penalty, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 describe lower bounds at the fixed point (the converged solution) of conservative policy evaluation. Theorem 3.1 applies to Equation (1) and gives a pointwise bound Q^π(s,a)≤Qπ(s,a)\hat Q^\pi(s,a)\leq Q^\pi(s,a)Q^​π(s,a)≤Qπ(s,a) on the stated domain. Theorem 3.2 applies to Equation (2) with μ=π\mu=\piμ=π and gives the tighter bound

V^π(s)=Ea∼π(⋅∣s)Q^π(s,a)≤Vπ(s).\hat V^\pi(s)=\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\hat Q^\pi(s,a) \leq V^\pi(s).V^π(s)=Ea∼π(⋅∣s)​Q^​π(s,a)≤Vπ(s).

To understand these two bounds, first isolate the exact Bellman-operator setting and fix the policy π\piπ. Define the state-action transition operator by

(Pπf)(s,a)=Es′∼P(⋅∣s,a), a′∼π(⋅∣s′)f(s′,a′).(P^\pi f)(s,a) =\mathbb E_{s'\sim P(\cdot|s,a),\,a'\sim\pi(\cdot|s')}f(s',a').(Pπf)(s,a)=Es′∼P(⋅∣s,a),a′∼π(⋅∣s′)​f(s′,a′).

For Equation (1), let h(s,a)=μ(a∣s)/π^β(a∣s)≥0h(s,a)=\mu(a|s)/\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)\geq0h(s,a)=μ(a∣s)/π^β​(a∣s)≥0. At the fixed point, the true and conservative Q-functions satisfy

Qπ=r+γPπQπ,Q^π=r+γPπQ^π−αh.\begin{aligned} Q^\pi&=r+\gamma P^\pi Q^\pi,\\ \hat Q^\pi&=r+\gamma P^\pi\hat Q^\pi-\alpha h. \end{aligned}QπQ^​π​=r+γPπQπ,=r+γPπQ^​π−αh.​

Subtracting the second equation from the first and unrolling gives

Qπ−Q^π=α∑t=0∞γt(Pπ)th≥0.Q^\pi-\hat Q^\pi =\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t(P^\pi)^t h \geq0.Qπ−Q^​π=αt=0∑∞​γt(Pπ)th≥0.

Each term is nonnegative because transition probabilities and hhh are nonnegative. This gives the pointwise bound and shows how the downward correction propagates through future steps.

For Equation (2) with μ=π\mu=\piμ=π, the correction is instead g(s,a)=π(a∣s)/π^β(a∣s)−1g(s,a)=\pi(a|s)/\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)-1g(s,a)=π(a∣s)/π^β​(a∣s)−1, which can be negative for individual actions. Its policy average is nevertheless nonnegative:

c(s)=Ea∼π(⋅∣s)g(s,a)=∑aπ(a∣s)2π^β(a∣s)−1=∑a[π(a∣s)−π^β(a∣s)]2π^β(a∣s)≥0.\begin{aligned} c(s)&=\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}g(s,a)\\ &=\sum_a\frac{\pi(a|s)^2}{\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)}-1\\ &=\sum_a\frac{[\pi(a|s)-\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)]^2} {\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)}\geq0. \end{aligned}c(s)​=Ea∼π(⋅∣s)​g(s,a)=a∑​π^β​(a∣s)π(a∣s)2​−1=a∑​π^β​(a∣s)[π(a∣s)−π^β​(a∣s)]2​≥0.​

The last equality follows by expanding the square and using ∑aπ(a∣s)=∑aπ^β(a∣s)=1\sum_a\pi(a|s)=\sum_a\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)=1∑a​π(a∣s)=∑a​π^β​(a∣s)=1. Thus c(s)c(s)c(s) is zero when the policies agree and increases with this squared distribution mismatch.

Let ΔV(s)=Vπ(s)−V^π(s)\Delta V(s)=V^\pi(s)-\hat V^\pi(s)ΔV(s)=Vπ(s)−V^π(s) and define the state transition operator (Pπv)(s)=Ea∼π, s′∼P(⋅∣s,a)v(s′)(P_\pi v)(s)=\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi,\,s'\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}v(s')(Pπ​v)(s)=Ea∼π,s′∼P(⋅∣s,a)​v(s′). Averaging the fixed-point difference over a∼π(⋅∣s)a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)a∼π(⋅∣s) yields

ΔV=αc+γPπΔV,ΔV=α∑t=0∞γtPπtc≥0.\begin{aligned} \Delta V&=\alpha c+\gamma P_\pi\Delta V,\\ \Delta V&=\alpha\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^tP_\pi^t c\geq0. \end{aligned}ΔVΔV​=αc+γPπ​ΔV,=αt=0∑∞​γtPπt​c≥0.​

This explains why the tighter objective preserves a lower bound on expected policy value even though some individual action values can increase. With an exact Bellman operator, any positive α\alphaα suffices for these evaluation bounds. With empirical rewards and transitions, the subtraction also contains sampling-error terms of either sign, so α\alphaα must provide a sufficient margin to dominate them.

Theorem 3.3 also gives a per-iteration lower-bound result under additional conditions. In particular, the learned policy must stay sufficiently close to the softmax policy induced by the current Q-function, so that policy mismatch does not remove the conservatism. Writing πQ^k(a∣s)∝eQ^k(s,a)\pi_{\hat Q^k}(a|s)\propto e^{\hat Q^k(s,a)}πQ^​k​(a∣s)∝eQ^​k(s,a) and bounding the total-variation distance between the improved policy and this softmax policy by ϵ\epsilonϵ, a sufficient condition in the exact-operator setting is

Ea∼πQ^k(⋅∣s)[πQ^k(a∣s)π^β(a∣s)−1]≥ϵmax⁡a:π^β(a∣s)>0πQ^k(a∣s)π^β(a∣s).\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi_{\hat Q^k}(\cdot|s)} \left[\frac{\pi_{\hat Q^k}(a|s)}{\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)}-1\right] \geq\epsilon\max_{a:\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)>0} \frac{\pi_{\hat Q^k}(a|s)}{\hat\pi_\beta(a|s)}.Ea∼πQ^​k​(⋅∣s)​[π^β​(a∣s)πQ^​k​(a∣s)​−1]≥ϵa:π^β​(a∣s)>0max​π^β​(a∣s)πQ^​k​(a∣s)​.

The conservative margin must therefore outweigh the policy mismatch.

These theoretical Q-iterations should be distinguished from individual optimizer steps. A Q-iteration solves the corresponding CQL optimization problem, while one gradient update only moves toward its solution. A lower bound is therefore not generally guaranteed after every optimizer step. Theorem 3.4 formalizes expansion of the expected Q-value gap between data and penalized actions. Theorems 3.5-3.6 characterize a behavior-divergence-penalized empirical return and high-probability safe policy improvement, with slack depending on sampling error and distribution shift.

Learning the policy

For continuous actions, a policy network πϕ(a∣s)\pi_\phi(a|s)πϕ​(a∣s) is trained using the learned Q-function QθQ_\thetaQθ​. Its objective is to maximize expected Q-value plus an entropy reward:

The loss is then used to update ϕ\phiϕ by gradient descent, with the Q-function parameters held fixed. Training alternates between updating the Q-function and updating the policy.

For continuous actions, alternatives to training a policy network include numerically maximizing Q(s,a)Q(s,a)Q(s,a) at each state or sampling from its normalized Boltzmann density. These alternatives incur extra computational cost.

Experimental results and baseline comparisons

The paper’s [1] D4RL results use normalized returns averaged over four seeds. On walker2d-medium-expert, CQL(H) scores 98.7 versus BC 11.3, BEAR 10.8, offline SAC 1.9, and BRAC-v 0.9 (Table 1), supporting its usefulness on mixed behavior data. On antmaze-medium-play it reaches 61.2 while all listed baselines score zero (Table 2), suggesting useful recombination of suboptimal trajectories. Improvements are not universal: halfcheetah-expert scores 104.8 versus BEAR 108.2 and BC 107.0; pen-cloned also favors BC. CQL(ρ\rhoρ) can be more stable on high-dimensional Adroit tasks.

The discrete Atari results support data efficiency: with 1% of replay data, Breakout returns are 61.1 for CQL, 11.0 for REM, and 7.9 for QR-DQN (Table 3). Table 4 tests conservatism directly: hopper-medium’s predicted-minus-true value is -7.48 for CQL(H), versus -156.70 for Eq. (1) and +4.32 for BEAR. This supports a tighter conservative estimate, while remaining empirical evidence on selected tasks rather than an unconditional neural-network guarantee.
